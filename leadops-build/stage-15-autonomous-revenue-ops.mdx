# Stage 15 — Autonomous Revenue Operations Layer

## 1. Purpose

Stage 15 operationalizes the predictive revenue capabilities established in Stages 7–9. The goal is a **policy-governed revenue operations control loop** that continuously evaluates opportunities, selects bounded next-best-actions, routes work, coordinates agents and humans, captures outcomes, and improves future decisions without bypassing authorization, consent, suppression, audit, or tenant boundaries.

```text
Signals / Events
      ↓
Prediction Layer
      ↓
Opportunity Prioritization
      ↓
Next-Best-Action Engine
      ↓
Policy / Consent / Suppression
      ↓
Routing / Assignment
      ↓
Workflow / Agent Execution
      ↓
Human Escalation where required
      ↓
Outcome / Revenue Event
      ↓
Attribution + Learning Loop
      ↺
```

The authoritative execution boundary remains:

```text
Prediction → NBA → Control Plane → Policy → Authorization → Execution → Audit
```

A model prediction is decision support, not authorization.

## 3. Revenue Operations Orchestrator

The orchestrator owns each revenue decision cycle:

1. consume eligible signals and outcomes
2. refresh opportunity state
3. calculate predictive features
4. rank opportunities
5. generate candidate actions
6. apply policy, consent, suppression, and capability gates
7. resolve action conflicts
8. route eligible work
9. schedule or execute through Stage 12 workflows
10. escalate actions requiring human intervention
11. record the immutable decision
12. capture execution and revenue outcomes
13. publish learning events

Every cycle is idempotent and restartable.

```ts
interface RevenueDecisionCycle {
  tenantId: string;
  cycleId: string;
  triggeredBy: "event" | "schedule" | "manual";
  opportunityIds: string[];
  startedAt: string;
  completedAt?: string;
}
```

## 4. Opportunity Prioritization

Core expected-revenue calculation:

```text
expected_revenue = deal_value × close_probability
```

A configurable priority function can incorporate freshness, contactability, SLA risk, strategic priority, and action cost:

```text
priority =
  expected_revenue
  × freshness_factor
  × contactability_factor
  × SLA_factor
  × strategic_priority
  ÷ action_cost
```

Coefficients are tenant-scoped configuration. Ranking must expose factor-level metadata rather than an opaque score.

```json
{
  "opportunityId": "opp_123",
  "expectedRevenue": 18400,
  "priorityScore": 0.91,
  "factors": {
    "dealValue": 23000,
    "closeProbability": 0.8,
    "freshness": 0.96,
    "contactability": 0.88,
    "slaRisk": 0.92
  },
  "expiresAt": "2026-09-25T20:00:00Z"
}
```

## 5. Next-Best-Action Engine

Candidate actions:

```text
CALL
SEND_EMAIL
SEND_SMS
REQUEST_ENRICHMENT
ASSIGN_HUMAN
SCHEDULE_FOLLOWUP
WAIT
SUPPRESS
```

The engine has three stages:

### Candidate generation
Generate actions supported by lead state, consent, channel availability, connector capabilities, and policy.

### Candidate evaluation
Evaluate expected revenue impact, execution probability, urgency, intent, SLA risk, contactability, channel suitability, action cost, contact history, frequency limits, and model confidence.

### Candidate authorization
Send the selected candidate through the Stage 9 policy/control plane.

A high model score never authorizes an action by itself.

Reason codes should expose observable factors such as `HIGH_INTENT`, `RECENT_PRICING_SIGNAL`, `SLA_RISK`, `HIGH_EXPECTED_REVENUE`, `CONTACTABLE`, `FOLLOWUP_DUE`, `RECENT_CONTACT`, `CONSENT_REQUIRED`, and `HUMAN_APPROVAL_REQUIRED`.

Do not expose hidden chain-of-thought or internal model reasoning.

## 7. Confidence and Autonomy

Autonomy is policy-controlled.

- **High confidence:** may auto-execute explicitly authorized low-risk actions such as internal enrichment, queue reprioritization, or approved scheduling.
- **Medium confidence:** may require approval based on action, value, channel, or tenant policy.
- **Low confidence:** routes to human review, enrichment, research, or WAIT.

Confidence never overrides consent, suppression, authorization, or hard policy blocks.

## 8. Intelligent Routing

Routing can use:

- territory
- owner relationship
- skills
- availability
- workload
- SLA
- opportunity value
- predicted close probability
- timezone
- permitted language preference
- account ownership
- business hours

Hard constraints eliminate invalid assignees. Soft constraints optimize among eligible assignees.

Every routing decision records applied constraints and the selected assignment. Fairness monitoring prevents eligible queues or owners from being systematically starved.

## 9. Follow-Up Scheduling

Required gates:

- consent where required
- suppression status
- channel eligibility
- quiet hours
- tenant business hours
- frequency caps
- recent-contact windows
- workflow availability
- recommendation expiration

Lifecycle:

```text
PROPOSED → POLICY_CHECK → SCHEDULED → DUE → EXECUTING → COMPLETED
```

If opportunity state changes, pending follow-ups must be re-evaluated before execution.

## 10. Appointment Optimization

Recommendations can consider customer availability, owner availability, timezone, appointment type, historical show patterns, urgency, SLA, capacity, and routing constraints.

The optimizer proposes slots but never bypasses calendar permissions. Recommendations retain their inputs and eventual outcomes.

## 11. Forecasting

Operational forecasts expose:

```text
Baseline
Conservative
Expected
Upside
```

Every run records period, opportunity population, model/version, assumptions, scenario parameters, predicted revenue, realized revenue when available, calibration metrics, and forecast error.

```text
forecast_error = realized_revenue - predicted_revenue
```

Track systematic overprediction and underprediction, not only aggregate accuracy.

## 12. Experimentation

Experiments support approved A/B assignments, holdouts, policy variants, workflow variants, timing experiments, and permitted channel experiments.

Each experiment requires:

- owner
- start/end time
- eligible population
- exclusions
- assignment strategy
- primary metric
- guardrails
- traffic/sample limits
- rollback behavior

Consent, suppression, communication policy, and connector permissions remain authoritative.

## 13. Revenue Agent Coordination

Multiple agents must not independently issue conflicting consequential actions for one opportunity.

```text
Opportunity
   ↓
Coordinator
   ├── Research Agent
   ├── Enrichment Agent
   ├── Qualification Agent
   ├── Scheduling Agent
   └── Follow-Up Agent
```

The coordinator owns sequencing, priority, opportunity locks, duplicate prevention, capability checks, agent health, escalation, and cancellation when state changes.

An agent recommendation becomes executable only through the Stage 9 control plane.

## 14. Human Escalation

Escalate when:

- confidence is below policy threshold
- expected value exceeds an approval threshold
- policy requires approval
- agents disagree
- the customer requests a human
- execution repeatedly fails
- an unusual state is detected
- a high-risk action is proposed

Record entity, proposed action, reason, policy, requester, risk, expiration, approver, decision, timestamp, and audit reference.

## 15. Closed-Loop Learning

```text
Decision
  ↓
Execution
  ↓
Customer / Revenue Outcome
  ↓
Attribution
  ↓
Model Evaluation
  ↓
Calibration
  ↓

  ↓
```

Outcome events include contact attempted, contact connected, response received, appointment booked/attended, opportunity advanced/lost, deal won, revenue recognized, suppression requested, consent changed, workflow failure, and human override.

Learning may update scoring parameters and model calibration only through approved model-management processes. It must never silently mutate authorization policy.

## 20. Failure Handling

Revenue automation fails closed for consequential actions.

- Prediction outage: use configured WAIT, deterministic fallback, or human review; never invent predictions.
- CRM outage: do not assume writes succeeded; retry idempotently and reconcile.
- Communication outage: preserve pending state and enforce provider retry limits.
- Calendar outage: do not claim an appointment exists.
- Policy outage: block consequential autonomous actions unless an explicitly configured fail-safe policy permits them.
- Agent timeout: release task leases safely and prevent duplicate execution through idempotency.

## 21. Governance Guardrails

Hard boundaries:

- no consent bypass
- no suppression bypass
- no tenant isolation bypass
- no hidden policy changes
- no financial commitments without authorization
- no uncontrolled outbound messaging
- no unbounded retries
- no autonomous alteration of authorization rules
- no model score as sole authorization
- no execution without capability scope
- no irreversible action without required approval
- no execution after recommendation expiration
- no silent deletion of decision or audit history

Kill switches exist at:

```text
Global
Tenant
Workflow
Connector
Agent
Action Type
Experiment
```

## 22. Conflict Resolution

When multiple systems propose actions for one opportunity:

```text
1. Hard policy
2. Consent / suppression
3. Tenant authorization
4. Control-plane locks
5. Active human instruction
6. System-of-record state
7. Deterministic rules
8. Verified predictive decision
9. Agent recommendation
10. Heuristic fallback
```

Only one consequential action should hold the execution lease unless action types are explicitly declared non-conflicting.

## 25. Acceptance Criteria

Stage 15 is complete when:

- opportunities can be ranked using inspectable factor metadata
- NBA candidates can be generated and evaluated
- policy and consent gates remain authoritative
- actions expire safely
- routing respects hard and soft constraints
- follow-ups respect quiet hours and frequency caps
- appointments are recommended without bypassing calendar authorization
- forecasts are versioned and calibrated against outcomes
- experiments have holdouts and rollback controls
- agents coordinate without duplicate consequential actions
- humans can approve or reject escalations
- outcomes feed attribution and calibration
- consequential decisions are auditable
- operations remain tenant-isolated
- required failure modes fail closed
- kill switches stop autonomous execution
- Command Center users can inspect decisions, actions, outcomes, and policy results

## 26. Definition of Done

The operational revenue path is now:

```text
Signal
  → Intelligence
  → Prediction
  → Opportunity
  → Next-Best-Action
  → Policy
  → Routing
  → Workflow / Agent
  → Human Escalation
  → Execution
  → Outcome
  → Attribution
  → Calibration
  → Optimization
```

> **Autonomy increases execution speed, not authorization authority.**

Stage 15 turns the earlier predictive capabilities into a production-oriented revenue operations loop while preserving Stage 9 as the control plane, Stage 12 as the workflow engine, Stage 13 as the operational command center, and Stage 14 as the production frontend.
